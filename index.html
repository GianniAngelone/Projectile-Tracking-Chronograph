Project Overview

Project Description

The Projectile Tracking Chronograph (PTC) is a senior design project focused on measuring projectile velocity and estimating trajectory using a combination of laser-based sensing, synchronized strobe illumination, embedded control, and image processing. Unlike traditional chronographs that only report velocity, PTC reports both the projectile's speed and its 3D trajectory, including launch angle and estimated range.

About the Team

Group 05 is an interdisciplinary senior design team at the University of Central Florida, composed of students in Optical Engineering, Electrical Engineering, and Computer Engineering. The team designed and built a practical and affordable system that combines multiple engineering disciplines into a single ballistic measurement platform.

My Role — Gianni Angelone

Software and UI Integration Lead (Computer Engineering). I wrote the software that connects the laser gate, strobes, cameras, microcontroller, and display into one automated measurement cycle.

  • Vision pipeline (Python, OpenCV, Raspberry Pi 5): each projectile image is compared against a reference frame taken under the same lighting, normalized, and thresholded. The projectile position is the centroid of the largest contour, found with image moments.
  • 3D trajectory: centroids from both cameras are converted to viewing angles using the calibrated focal lengths and triangulated into an (x, y, z) position. Two strobe exposures give two 3D points, which produce the horizontal angle, vertical launch angle, and estimated range.
  • ESP32-S3 firmware (MicroPython): fires 100 µs strobe pulses, samples the capacitor-bank voltage on the ADC before and after each flash, selects strobe banks, and reads the priority-encoder fault lines to identify a failed strobe module.
  • System integration: the ESP32-S3 and Raspberry Pi 5 communicate over UART, so a laser-gate trigger starts image capture automatically and results appear on the display.
18.7 m/s average measured speed vs. 18.58 m/s independent reference (10 trials)
2 cameras stereo triangulation for full 3D position
100 µs strobe pulses timed from the laser gate
